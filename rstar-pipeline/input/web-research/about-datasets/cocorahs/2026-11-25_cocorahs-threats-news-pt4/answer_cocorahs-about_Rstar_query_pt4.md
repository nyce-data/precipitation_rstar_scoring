# CoCoRaHS, the Colorado Climate Center and its data systems: funding, staff and access, January 2025 to November 2026

**Overall rating: no current threat found to CoCoRaHS's funding, staff or data access, with two emerging risks.** The largest change is a loss of people, not money. Founder Nolan Doesken died on 15 November 2026. He had retired from paid roles in 2017, and no one has been named to replace him. One NSF education grant naming CoCoRaHS as a partner was terminated in April 2025. The two emerging risks: CSU's FY2027 cuts aren't tied to any unit, and the National Mesonet Program contract was recompeted in 2026 with no record of whether CoCoRaHS is still paid under it. Donations rose to a record $351,517, the data site stayed up, and downstream copies kept flowing. **Confidence: moderate.** The "no threat" findings rest on absence of evidence, checked in the organizations' own records and with two search engines.

## Funding

- **NSF: one termination, not of operating money.**
  - Award 2313996, an NC State–led participatory-science program, names CoCoRaHS as one of its two partner projects. CSU's Russ Schumacher is a co-PI.
  - NSF's record reads "Administratively Terminated Award", with an end date of 04/18/2025. The award is on NSF's own terminated-awards list ([NSF Award API](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=CoCoRaHS); [NSF terminated awards](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/NSF-Terminated-Awards.csv)).
  - It paid for education research, not for running the network. CSU's share isn't stated, and no reinstatement was found. **High** confidence that it ended; its effect on CoCoRaHS is likely small.
  - Schumacher's own NSF research award runs to January 2028 ([NSF API](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?pdPIName=Schumacher&awardeeName=Colorado%20State%20University&dateStart=01/01/2024)). No NSF award names the CCC.
- **National Mesonet Program (NMP), CoCoRaHS's last known federal payment route** (**moderate** on the figures, **low** on what they mean for CoCoRaHS):
  - FY2026 enacted: $30,000,000 ([CJS joint explanatory statement, Jan 2026](https://appropriations.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/republicans-appropriations.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/FY26-CJS-JES.pdf)).
  - FY2027 committee levels, not enacted: $40,000,000 from the House ([H. Rept. 119-652](https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt652/CRPT-119hrpt652.pdf)) and $35,000,000 from the Senate ([Senate CJS statement](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/cjs_explanatory_stmt.pdf)).
  - NOAA's new contract began September 1, 2026. It extends the KBR partnership, with KBR as prime contractor working with "about 70" private industry partners ([NOAA, 2026-08-27](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-boosts-weather-data-collection-capabilities-with-new-mesonet-contract)). The contract is a $1.1 billion-ceiling IDIQ running through August 2031, with Synoptic as lead subcontractor ([Washington Technology, 2026-06-11](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/06/kbr-wins-11b-weather-data-collection-recompete/414128/); [Synoptic, 2026-09-24](https://synopticdata.com/news/synoptic-secures-lead-subcontractor-role-on-noaas-five-year-1-1b-ceiling-national-mesonet-program-contract)).
  - None of these names CoCoRaHS, and no appropriations text does. NMP's undated partner list still does ([NMP partners](https://nationalmesonet.us/cocorahs/)).
  - If CoCoRaHS lost NMP pay, HQ would lose revenue. NWS access to the reports would continue: they reach the NWS through CoCoRaHS's own feeds and MADIS ([MADIS providers](https://madis.ncep.noaa.gov/hydro_providers.shtml)).
- **Donations are the documented mainstay** (**high**: CSU's own pages, read raw):
  - The 2024 drive raised $279,040 (124% of a $225,000 goal) from 2,868 donors and ended January 20, 2025 ([CSU GiveCampus, 2024 drive](https://give.colostate.edu/cocorahs-2024)).
  - The 2025 drive closed at $351,517 (117%) from 3,081 donors ([CSU GiveCampus list](https://give.colostate.edu/)).
    - The mid-January 2026 snapshot showed $329,222, with 3,055 donors in the header and "2,984+ donors" in HQ's note, "to help sustain the network in 2026" ([2025 drive](https://give.colostate.edu/cocorahs-2025)).
    - The live list is final; the snapshot is an earlier tally.
  - The 2026 drive had $17,857 on 25 November 2026 and officially starts on December 1.
  - HQ says gifts support "data management, web development work" among other things ([The Catch, Dec 2025](https://mailchi.mp/cocorahs/newsletter_the-catch_dec-2025-happy-holidays), read via WebFetch).
- **CSU and state support.**
  - The sponsor page still lists the Colorado Agricultural Experiment Station (1998–present), and also NOAA (2006–present) and NSF (2010–present), with no amounts ([CoCoRaHS sponsors](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=sponsors)). The NOAA and NSF entries don't match the earlier runs' finding that those grants ended in 2015 and 2017; the list may be stale (**low** confidence).
  - Colorado's 2025 Water Projects Bill gave $200,000 to the CCC's Colorado Mesonet, which is a separate network ([CWCB summary of SB25-283](https://spl.cde.state.co.us/artemis/nrserials/nr326internet/nr3262025internet.pdf)).

## Staff

- **Founder's death.** CoCoRaHS's memorial page says Doesken died at home in Fort Collins ([CoCoRaHS memorial](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=nolanmemorial)). Its message page gives the date as "Sunday, November 15th" ([CoCoRaHS HQ](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=mod&mod=1)), and MPR News also says Nov. 15, though BizWest says he died "Saturday" ([BizWest, 2026-11-16](https://bizwest.com/2026/11/16/csu-climatologist-doesken-dead-after-parkinsons-battle/), read via WebFetch); this answer relies on CoCoRaHS's own notice ([MPR News, 2026-11-21](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/11/21/precipitation-collection-network-pioneer-nolan-doesken-remembered-by-weather-community)).
  - The CCC says he was state climatologist and its director from 2006 to 2017 ([CCC](https://climate.colostate.edu/nolan.html)).
  - CSU names Schumacher and national coordinator Henry Reges as the current leaders ([CSU Source, 2026-11-19](https://source.colostate.edu/csu-atmospheric-community-remembers-nolan-doesken/)). None of these pages names a successor or a change in funding (**high**).
  - For P-2, this removes the retirement-eligible founder but leaves no paid post empty. Part 3's age-based estimate for Steve Hilberg still stands.
- **Paid staff unchanged.**
  - CoCoRaHS's contact page lists the same US staff in December 2024 and November 2026; only the Canadian coordinator changed ([contact page, 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20241210093302/https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=contactus); [contact page, now](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=contactus)).
  - On the CCC staff page, Becky Bolinger and Alistair Vierod were gone by January 2025, with no reason given. An Engagement Climatologist was added in 2025, and Hilberg and a CoAgMET technician appear by December 2025 ([CCC, Jan 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250114215449/https://climate.colostate.edu/ccc_info.html); [CCC, Dec 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20251223180953/https://climate.colostate.edu/ccc_info.html)).
  - The department's December 2025 newsletter says CoCoRaHS "continues to thrive" and mentions "federal funding headwinds", but reports no cuts ([Atmospheric Science newsletter, 2025](https://www.atmos.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-CSU-Atmospheric-Science-Newsletter.pdf)). **Moderate** confidence: staff lists, not payroll.
- **CSU-wide cuts (context, emerging).**
  - January 2025: deans were asked to model 3%, 5% and 7% reductions ([CSU, 2025-01-23](https://president.colostate.edu/speeches-and-writing/budget-update-1-23-2025/)).
  - April 2026: CSU's own plan has $35.8 million of strategic cuts, $3 million from positions expected to fall vacant and $15 million of one-time funds. CSU says "less than 1 percent of our workforce will be affected" ([CSU, 2026-04-13](https://president.colostate.edu/speeches-and-writing/budget-update-and-next-steps/)).
  - The press figures are $35.8 million ([Higher Ed Dive, 2026-05-11](https://www.highereddive.com/news/colorado-state-university-turns-to-layoffs-and-other-cuts-to-manage-budget/819906/)) and about $54 million with "160 vacant and filled positions" cut ([Chalkbeat, 2026-08-07](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/08/07/colorado-public-colleges-and-universities-budget-challenges/)). CSU's three parts add up to about $53.8 million, which likely explains the gap. That reading is ours; no source says so.
  - The faculty union reported a recommended cut of 178 FTE positions and said unit lists weren't shared ([AAUP-CSU, May 2026](https://aaupcsu.org/aaup-csu-newsletter-may-2026/)). CSU's 1 percent, the union's 178 and Chalkbeat's 160 measure different things and aren't reconciled.
  - No source ties any of these cuts to Atmospheric Science, the CCC or CoCoRaHS.
- **P-4 (one person is a point of failure).** The October 2025 Catch speaks of "our web developer", singular, and no second developer or job posting was found ([The Catch, Oct 2025](https://mailchi.mp/cocorahs/newsletter_the-catch_oct-2025-new-water-year)). This weakly supports P-4 (**low**).
- **NWS partnership continues** (**moderate**).
  - NOAA calls CoCoRaHS "the single largest source of daily precipitation data used by the National Weather Service", and its spring 2025 rally added 1,196 volunteers. Counts of active observers vary by date: "more than 25,000" in July 2025 ([The Current](https://thecurrentga.org/2025/07/08/nationwide-volunteer-program-recruits-backyard-weather-observers/)) and "over 28,000" in November 2026 ([CCC](https://climate.colostate.edu/nolan.html)). In March 2025 CoCoRaHS said it supplies "3/4 (76%) of the snow water equivalent data used by NOAA" ([The Catch, 2025-03-04](https://mailchi.mp/cocorahs/newsletter_the-catch_march_2025)) ([NOAA, 2025](https://www.noaa.gov/office-education/citizen-science/news/rain-gauge-rally-recruits-new-volunteers-to-measure-rain-and-snow)).
  - Weather offices ran CoCoRaHS recruiting in 2025 and 2026 ([NWS Paducah, 2026](https://www.weather.gov/pah/CoCoRaHS_Volunteers)).
  - No source says an office cut CoCoRaHS recruiting or training because of the 2025 staff losses.
  - Congress raised NWS funding 8% for FY2026 and directed it to "maintain staffing levels" ([CRS IF13024, 2026-03-12](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13024)).

## Access

- **No outage, login, rate limit, export limit or data removal found** (D-3 contradicted; **moderate**, since Wayback can't see a short outage).
  - Wayback captures of www.cocorahs.org (daily, 2025 to 18 Nov 2026), data.cocorahs.org and maps.cocorahs.org show no error codes ([Wayback CDX](https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=www.cocorahs.org/&from=2025&to=2026&collapse=timestamp:8&fl=original,timestamp,statuscode&output=json)).
  - CoCoRaHS's July 2025 multistate report gives "5+ million records per month … 99+ percent uptime" and a move of its whole cyberinfrastructure to Azure ([NIMSS WCC1012, 2025-07-21](https://nimss.org/seas/52786)).
  - In November 2026 HQ launched a "brand-new" mapping system, MapHub ([CoCoRaHS HQ](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=mod&mod=1)).
  - Downstream copies were current in late November 2026: GHCN-Daily version 3.35-upd-2026112318 ([NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/ghcn/daily/ghcnd-version.txt)), RCC-ACIS station records valid through 2026-11-25 ([ACIS](https://data.rcc-acis.org/StnMeta?county=44005&meta=name,sids,valid_daterange&elems=pcpn)), and MADIS.
- **Political pressure: none found** (D-5 contradicted; **moderate**).
  - In March 2025, after volunteers wrote in about federal changes, CoCoRaHS said it "is not part of NOAA, rather, we are extremely close partners" ([The Catch, 2025-03-04](https://mailchi.mp/cocorahs/newsletter_the-catch_march_2025)).
  - The CCC kept publishing climate-change material through 2026 ([CCC news](https://climate.colostate.edu/ccc_news.html)).
  - NOAA's 2025 product retirements did not include CoCoRaHS ([FlowingData list, 2025-04-22](https://flowingdata.com/2025/04/22/list-of-noaa-datasets-to-be-discontinued/)).
- **One unexplained note.** The July 2025 report says many participants missed the May 2025 meeting "due to constraints and uncertainty", and it lists no grants.
