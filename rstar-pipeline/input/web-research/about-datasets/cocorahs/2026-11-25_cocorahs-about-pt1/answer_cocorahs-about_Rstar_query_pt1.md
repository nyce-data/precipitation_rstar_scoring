# CoCoRaHS daily precipitation observations as a dataset (Part 1: hosts, inputs, organizations)

CoCoRaHS's daily precipitation reports live in one database run by CoCoRaHS headquarters at the Colorado Climate Center, Colorado State University. That database is the archive of record. CoCoRaHS serves it through its own sites (cocorahs.org ViewData, the data.cocorahs.org Export Manager, the Data Explorer, Water Year Summaries and maps). NOAA NCEI's GHCN-Daily receives a daily stream and is the only independent archived copy. Everything else (ACIS and the state climatologists that use it, MADIS, the Iowa Environmental Mesonet, NWS offices) is downstream of one of those two. The dataset has no DOI or version number. The export route is public (CC BY 4.0, "as is") but undocumented. About 28,000 volunteers read the same 4-inch manual gauge at about 7 a.m. On May 1, 2026 there were 27,623 active stations: 26,758 in the USA, 833 in Canada and 32 in the Bahamas. **Overall confidence: moderate.** Hosting and copies are well documented. Server location, backups and observer counts by country (other than Canada) are not published.

- **Archive of record (moderate).** CoCoRaHS headquarters sits at the Colorado Climate Center on CSU's Foothills Campus ([CoCoRaHS Contact Us](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=contactus)), and CSU says CoCoRaHS is "Currently housed at the Colorado Climate Center" ([Colorado Climate Center](https://climate.colostate.edu/resources.html)). A WGRFC paper says reports are "recorded in a central archive at CoCoRaHS headquarters and made available to the public in near-real time" ([Texas Water Journal, WGRFC](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/twj/twj-7071.pdf)). No page says whether the servers are on campus or with a cloud provider, or whether CoCoRaHS keeps a backup.
- **The NCEI copy (high).** CoCoRaHS's QA/QC document says "All data submitted into CoCoRaHS since 1998 are continually streamed to NCDC". New values "are retrieved by GHCN-Daily on a daily basis for existing stations" ([CoCoRaHS QA/QC document](https://www.cocorahs.org/)). NCEI's 2022 slide "CoCoRaHS Data Flow To NCEI" shows CoCoRaHS Web Services feeding the NCEI Archive, GHCN-Daily QC and the RCCs/RFCs ([NCEI, WERA-1012 2022](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA1012_2022_NCEI.pdf)). GHCN-Daily "Includes 133 million CoCoRaHS observations (over all elements) for 1998-present" ([NCEI, WERA 2024](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA_2024_7_NOAA_NCEI_Durre.pdf)). Stations need "at least 100 individual data values" to be included, so new or short-lived stations are only at CoCoRaHS. New stations are added to GHCN-Daily "once a month, so depending on timing it could be four months before a new station appears" there and in ACIS ([CoCoRaHS, Other Ways to View Your Data](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=Resources_viewingdata)).
- **Terms, DOI, version (moderate).** The data usage policy says data "are made available to the public via the web site" under CC BY 4.0 and "provided 'as is'" ([CoCoRaHS Data Usage Policy](https://www.cocorahs.org/content.aspx?page=datausagepolicy)). CoCoRaHS's own 2025 slides say "Data is free to download and use" ([NRCC webinar](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/workshops/webinars/2025/04/present_1.pdf)). There is no API documentation, no stability or rate-limit statement, and no data version. NOAA's catalog record gives "Dataset_DOI N/A" ([NOAA DIF record](https://paleo.ltrr.arizona.edu/dif/noaa-other-1002736.xml)), and the first 25 DataCite hits for "CoCoRaHS" are other parties' works ([DataCite](https://api.datacite.org/dois?query=CoCoRaHS&page[size]=25)). GHCN-Daily's own version and DOI are the only versioned route for these data.

## What goes into the dataset (q2)

- **Volunteers (moderate).** The home page gave "more than 28,000 active observers in the United States, Canada, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, and the Bahamas" when captured 2026-11-18 ([CoCoRaHS home](https://www.cocorahs.org/)). Earlier figures are "27,400+ volunteers" in April 2025 ([NRCC webinar](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/workshops/webinars/2025/04/present_1.pdf)) and "over 25,000 observers" on an NWS page from 2025 ([NWS Detroit](https://www.weather.gov/dtx/CoCoRaHS)). These read as growth plus rounding, not a conflict.
- **Stations.** The Data Explorer lists 25,106 stations with a report between 08/27/2026 and 11/25/2026 ([DEx](https://dex.cocorahs.org/)).
- **By country (moderate).** CoCoRaHS lists three countries: United States, Canada and the Bahamas ([Countries](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=countries)). Puerto Rico, the USVI and Guam are counted under the USA.

  | Active stations (reported in the prior 12 months) | May 1, 2026 | 5-year change |
  |---|---|---|
  | All | 27,623 (27,676 on May 1, 2025) | +3,374 (13.9%) since May 1, 2021 |
  | USA (incl. PR, USVI, Guam) | 26,758 | +3,349 (14.3%) |
  | Canada | 833 | +11 (1.3%) |
  | Bahamas | 32 | +14 (77.8%) |

  Source: CoCoRaHS station history report of May 1, 2026, as given in a state coordinator's slides ([CoCoRaHS WCC 2026, Spies](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WCC_2026_Day2_Spies_Recruiting_Retention.pdf), "Active Stations" and "A long view of the past 5 years" slides). The same slides give 82.7M daily precipitation reports by the end of 2025. Reporting observers grew 1.2% in 2025 and 86% over 16 years.

  Turnover is high. In the year to May 1, 2026, 4,787 stations were created (USA 4,578, Canada 139, Bahamas 3), but only 2,604 sent a first report, for a net change of −53. Another 3,245 stations last reported in 2024 (same slides).

  Older totals mark the growth: "nearly 15,000 volunteers" in 2010 ([CSU 2010](https://newsmediarelations.colostate.edu/2010/11/22/colorado-state-university-gets-funding-to-improve-national-precipitation-monitoring-network/)), and "more than 62,000 volunteers have joined" by 2018, which counts everyone who ever joined, not active observers ([WGRFC paper](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/twj/twj-7071.pdf)).

  Observer counts by country exist only for Canada: 80 active observers in its first year (from 2011) and 843 "As of January 31, 2023" ([House of Commons SRSR 28](https://www.ourcommons.ca/DocumentViewer/en/44-1/SRSR/meeting-28/evidence)). The 27,623 active stations at 12 months and the 25,106 at 90 days are both stations, not observers.
  - Bahamas: joined "In May 2016", not 2013 ([CoCoRaHS Bahamas](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=welcomebh)).
  - Puerto Rico joined in 2014 and the USVI in 2015 ([CoCoRaHS 20 years](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=20years)).
- **History.** Active volunteers grew "from a few hundred in 2003 to over 2,000 by the summer of 2004" ([CoCoRaHS 20 years](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=20years)).
- **Gauge (high).** Every observer must use the CoCoRaHS 4-inch-diameter high-capacity manual gauge, and automated gauges are not accepted. Its collection efficiency is 101–105% of the NWS 8-inch standard gauge ([CoCoRaHS, Why manual measurements, Rev. 11/2024](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/Why%20CoCoRaHS%20requires%20manual%20measurements.pdf); [Cifelli et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-86-8-1069), which names the maker as Productive Alternatives, Minnesota, in 2005).
- **Entry and updating (high).** The 24-hour total is read "EACH MORNING ~7 AM" and entered through the CoCoRaHS app or the online form. Observations are "immediately available for the public to view and use" ([NRCC webinar](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/workshops/webinars/2025/04/present_1.pdf)), and maps update through the day for late reports ([Cifelli et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-86-8-1069)). On 2026-11-18, 6,379 daily reports had arrived by 9:10 a.m. EST ([CoCoRaHS home](https://www.cocorahs.org/)). An overflowing gauge is reported as "11.00 inches" with a comment ([CoCoRaHS QA/QC document](https://www.cocorahs.org/)). So the archive changes continuously. NCEI pulls daily, and GHCN-Daily adds new stations monthly.
- **Measured and derived values.**
  - Direct measurements: the daily 24-hour precipitation (rain or melted snow), and new and total snow depth with snow water equivalent.
  - Multi-day accumulation reports: a separate report type, one measured total over several days. In ACIS, the days inside a multi-day period show as "M".
  - Derived: Water Year Summaries, monthly station summaries and maps.
  - The home page counts Daily, Multi-day, SigWx, Hail, Condition and ET reports separately, and the Export Manager exports Daily, Multi-Day, Hail and SigWx separately.
  - Hail, condition-monitoring, soil-moisture and evapotranspiration reports are parallel report types from the same observers. They are not part of the daily precipitation series. Condition monitoring is "supported by NIDIS" and used by Drought Monitor authors ([NIDIS 2019](https://www.drought.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/CoCoRaHsConditionMonitoring_Nov2019_508.pdf)).

## Who makes it (q3)

- **Colorado Climate Center, CSU (produce and host; high).** The network originated there in 1998 and is run from its headquarters ([About Us](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=aboutus)). The site footer reads "Copyright © 1998-2025, Colorado Climate Center".
- **Sponsors (pay; high).**
  - About Us: "NOAA and the National Science Foundation (NSF) have been major sponsors."
  - The sponsors page lists NOAA (2006–present), NSF and NWS, CSU units, utilities and water districts ([Sponsors](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=sponsors)).
  - NSF Grant 0229723 and UCAR COMET supported it in 2005 ([Cifelli et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-86-8-1069)).
  - NOAA's Office of Education has funded it through Environmental Literacy Grants since December 2006 ([CSU 2010](https://newsmediarelations.colostate.edu/2010/11/22/colorado-state-university-gets-funding-to-improve-national-precipitation-monitoring-network/)), and also funded the Data Explorer ([DEx about](https://dex.cocorahs.org/about)).
- **Coordinators (produce).** Volunteer county, regional and state coordinators recruit and train observers and "Quality control data routinely and often" ([county coordinator role](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=coord_in_counties)). Their employers were not found.
- **Users.**
  - River forecast centers: ABRFC uses the gauges as "ground truth" for radar estimates in its river models ([ABRFC](https://www.weather.gov/abrfc/cocorahs)). WGRFC "receives around 1,000 CoCoRaHS observervations [sic] … per day" and uses them to adjust its MPE ([WGRFC paper](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/twj/twj-7071.pdf)).
  - Forecast offices: NWS Raleigh uses them in daily precipitation maps ([NWS RAH](https://www.weather.gov/rah/cocorahs)).
  - METplus has a PointStat use case that reads a CoCoRaHS CSV through a Python-embedding reader ([METplus](https://metplus.readthedocs.io/en/main_v6.0/generated/model_applications/precipitation/PointStat_fcstURMA_obsCOCORAHS_ASCIIprecip.html); read through WebFetch).
  - NCEI uses the data in GHCN-Daily, which is about 40% of its entries (earlier run 2026-11-21_ghcn-daily-sources-pt5).

## Against the R* statements

| Statement | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| RRS-1 "two or more geographically independent copies" (strength) | **Partly supports.** The CoCoRaHS database (Fort Collins) and NCEI's GHCN-Daily (Asheville, moving to AWS) are run by different organizations in different places. But GHCN-Daily omits stations with fewer than 100 values and the non-precipitation report types, and NCEI's own copies stay near Asheville (earlier run 2026-11-23_ghcn-daily-repository-resilience-pt9). No CoCoRaHS backup is documented | c1, c4 |
| D-2 "storage is a single point of failure" (risk) | **Supports, for the full dataset.** The full record (all report types, every station and the latest days) exists only in CoCoRaHS's own database. No second site or backup is published. The core daily precipitation series is protected by the NCEI copy | c1, c3, c4 |
| DIRS-3 "depends on external data feeds" (risk) | **Contradicts for CoCoRaHS itself, supports for copies.** CoCoRaHS's input is its own volunteers' manual reports, with no outside feed. GHCN-Daily, ACIS, state climatologists, MADIS, RFCs and METplus all depend on the CoCoRaHS feed | c4, c5, c7, c10 |
| DIRS-4 "needs outside processing infrastructure" (risk) | **Weakly supports.** Production runs on CoCoRaHS's own system at CSU (a host institution, not a separate service). Its maps depend on OpenStreetMap/CARTO tiles. Long-term archiving and the versioned product depend on NCEI | c1, c2, c4 |

## Gaps

- **Server location and backups.** Where the CoCoRaHS database and websites physically run, and whether a backup exists, is not published. CoCoRaHS holds this; ask info@cocorahs.org.
- **Counts.** There are no observer counts by country except Canada, no PR/USVI/Guam station split, and no year-by-year series beyond the growth percentages above. CoCoRaHS holds these in its station history report, and the Data Explorer's filters would give station splits. Reges et al. 2016 (BAMS, AMS returned 403) likely has the history.
- **Other CoCoRaHS sites.** maphub.cocorahs.org (a newer map site) and the older data.cocorahs.org/cocorahs/maps/ appeared in search results only and were not read.
- **Export details.** The export URL format, its limits, `exportstations.aspx`, HADS ingest and re3data were not checked.
- **Pages not read.** The WERA 2024 DEx slides have no text layer, and the coordinator list loads by JavaScript and showed no names in the browser. `check-by-hand.md` lists 4 pages to open by hand.
- **Sites to trust.** Five hosts were refused by the sandbox (`sites-to-trust.md`); you can check whether to add any to your allowed domains.
