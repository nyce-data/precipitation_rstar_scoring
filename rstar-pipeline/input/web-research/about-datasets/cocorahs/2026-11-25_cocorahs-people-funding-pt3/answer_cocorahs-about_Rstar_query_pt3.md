# CoCoRaHS: the people who run it, its paid staff, and how it is paid for (Part 3)

CoCoRaHS is run by a paid staff of about five to seven people at Colorado State University's Colorado Climate Center. The State Climatologist, Russ Schumacher, directs the center, and founder Nolan Doesken is now emeritus. Three of the staff have been there since 2003–2004: Henry Reges (national coordinator), Julian Turner (the only web developer) and Noah Newman (education). The other two are Steve Hilberg (project manager and QA/QC), who is already past retirement age, and Dani Talmadge (QC). Federal grants ended in 2015 (NOAA) and 2017 (NSF). Since then the network has run on about $0.4–0.6 million a year from data users, donations and the NOAA-funded National Mesonet Program. No written succession plan was found. **Confidence: moderate.** Roles and funding are well documented; ages and retirement eligibility are estimates.

**Retirement rule (q3).** CSU's rules are published, so FERS does not apply:
- Faculty, administrative professionals and non-classified staff must join CSU's Defined Contribution Plan, or PERA only in limited cases. CSU contributes 12% ([CSU HR](https://hr.colostate.edu/current-employees/benefits/afap/retirement/)).
- CSU counts someone as a "retiree" at "Age 55 or greater with at least 20 years of 'service'" or "Age 60 or greater with at least 5 years" ([CSU HR, preparing for retirement](https://hr.colostate.edu/preparing-for-retirement/)).
- Phased (transitional) retirement is for tenured faculty only ([CSU Faculty Manual E.2.1.6](https://facultycouncil.colostate.edu/faculty-manual-section-e/)).
- PERA members appointed on or before 30 June 2005 count as CSU retirees once they are eligible under PERA's own rules and have five consecutive years at CSU. That case could apply to Reges, Newman and Turner, who were hired in 2003–2004, if they are in PERA.
- PERA's own age-and-service tables, by hire date ([Colorado PERA fact sheet](https://content.copera.org//wp-content/uploads/2024/11/colorado-pera-and-dps-benefit-structures-fact-sheet.pdf)), apply only to staff who are in PERA. Which plan each person is in is not published.

**What this means for the risks.**
- **P-2 is supported for Hilberg** (low confidence: ages estimated). He leads the day-to-day QC that Part 2 found rests mostly on one person.
- **P-2 is contradicted for Reges, Newman, Turner and Schumacher** (low confidence).
- Doesken is already retired. The risk with him is his availability, not his eligibility.

## Team, backup and succession (q4)

- **Size and tenure.** The paid core is small and long-serving.
  - The 2012–2017 agendas list six HQ staff ([2012](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA%201012%20-%202012Agenda.pdf); [2017](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA1012-2017FinalAgenda.pdf)).
  - In 2021 Reges described "a core staff now of about five of us that have been with the program over 10 years" ([Come Rain or Shine podcast, 2021](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1136681/8628856-cocorahs-citizen-science-in-weather-reporting), read as an extract).
  - HQ staff were 56% of FY2020's ~$420K spending ([WERA1012 minutes 2020–22](https://nimss.org/storage/3841/WERA1012_Summary_of_Meeting_Minutes_2020-2022.pdf)). The FY22 projection was $301,780 (51%) ([WERA 2022 slides](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA1012_2022_Budget.pdf)). FTEs and hire dates are not published. **Moderate.**
- **P-4 is supported for IT.** The 2021 roadmap says "One developer => 50 Work weeks a year". A vendor (Solliance, $150 an hour) helps with operations ([Turner, WERA 2021](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/Turner-WERA21.pdf)). In 2018 Turner said more people were ideally needed for the website, database, apps and system administration ([WERA 2018 executive summary](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA1012_2018ExSum.pdf)). **High.**
- **P-4 is supported for coordination and fundraising.** Reges is the national coordinator, the fundraising contact and the person behind the NIDIS support. No deputy or backup is named for him, or for anyone else. **Moderate** (no named backup is not proof that there is none).
- **P-1 is supported.** No written succession, workforce or knowledge-transfer plan was found in the WERA1012 agendas (2012–2024), notes, minutes or the 2019–2024 project outline ([NIMSS outline, via Wayback](https://nimss.org/projects/view/mrp/outline/18627)).
  - The outline's sustainability goal is about partnerships, money and people for the volunteer networks.
  - The 2015 Sustainability Committee was "Funding mostly" ([WERA 2015 notes](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA-1012-2015%20Meeting%20Notes.pdf)).
  - The only knowledge-transfer trace is that Turner "keeps detailed notes" on priorities. **Moderate.**
- **The founder's retirement was a phased handover, not a written plan.**
  - Doesken retired in August 2017 after 40 years at CSU.
  - Climate Center slides said he would be back in October, "primarily to work on CoCoRaHS" ([Goble, Sept 2017](https://mountainscholar.org/bitstreams/577906e2-f9d0-4e09-bc23-94055d6d0a67/download)).
  - CSU ran a search, and Schumacher became State Climatologist on 6 October 2017 ([CSU Source, 2017](https://source.colostate.edu/new-state-climatologist-challenge-colorados-fascinating-diverse-climate/)).
  - Doesken still reported to the committee in 2018 and 2020, and by 2024 he was "Founder/Emeritus". **High.**
  - This contradicts P-4 for the founder: the network outlived his retirement. It also shows that Hilberg's arrival from the Midwestern Regional Climate Center was the main way experience was brought in.

**What this means for D-1 (baseline only).**
- **D-1 is supported** (moderate). CoCoRaHS has had no direct federal grant since 2017. NOAA funding "ended in September" 2015 ([2015 letter](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=CoCoRaHSLetter15)), and in 2016 "The future of CoCoRaHS was very much in doubt" ([2016 letter](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=cocoletter16)). Most revenue comes from year-to-year payments and gifts. FY2020 expected a shortfall of about $20K, and some municipal funding was lost in FY2021.
- **D-1 is contradicted as "defunded"** (moderate). Revenue grew from about $400K in FY20 to $598K projected in FY22, and the National Mesonet Program route is NOAA money.
- **The sponsors page misleads.** It lists NOAA "2006-present" and NSF "2010 - present", but those entries are unchanged since a December 2014 snapshot ([sponsors page](https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=sponsors); [Wayback 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20141202023708/http://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=sponsors)). This conflict is resolved in favour of the award records.
