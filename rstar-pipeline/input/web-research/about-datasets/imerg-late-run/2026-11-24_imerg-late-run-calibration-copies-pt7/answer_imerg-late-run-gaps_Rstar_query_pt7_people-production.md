# IMERG Late Run: calibration, current inputs, alternatives and copies (Part 7, Run 2)

**The Late Run's calibration is kept by NASA's own people:** the GPM X-CAL working group, led at Goddard by Rachael Kroodsma, publishes the tables in a current L1C ATBD (v1.11, August 2026). GMI is the only reference, and no backup is named. The Late Run's gauge calibration is a set of climatological coefficients computed once, in 2024. **What the near-real-time stream ingests after the 2026 feed losses is not published anywhere public.** GEOS FP did not end; only FP-IT was replaced, by GEOS-IT. Several near-real-time datasets could stand in for the Late Run. **No current copy of the Late Run outside PPS and GES DISC was found.** Overall confidence: moderate. Many of the answers are "not published", and only the Late Run's file headers (behind a PPS sign-up) would settle them.

This run adds to the earlier IMERG runs (pt1–pt6) and the 2026-11-18 NASA satellite run; it does not repeat their findings.

## Calibration

- **Who keeps the X-CAL tables.** A NASA Goddard bio page says Kroodsma (Code 615) "leads the Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) inter-calibration working group (XCAL)". The working group "is responsible for delivering the intercalibration tables … to create the Level 1C dataset" ([NASA GSFC, n.d.](https://science.gsfc.nasa.gov/sci/bio/rachael.a.kroodsma)). The L1C ATBD names the "GPM Intercalibration (X-CAL) Working Group", not a person or institution ([PPS, L1C ATBD v1.11, 2026](https://arthurhou.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/Documents/L1C_ATBD_GPM_V08.pdf)).
  - GSICS has only an old copy: Wes Berg's (CSU) "PMM XCAL Intercalibration tables for Level 1C data (V05)", dated 11 Dec 2018 and read from a saved copy, since gsics.atmos.umd.edu refused connections ([GSICS wiki, 2019, Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20250523022002/https://gsics.atmos.umd.edu/bin/view/Development/Microwave-Deliverable)). So GSICS hosts no current tables and does not maintain them.
  - **Funding: not found.** No award names X-CAL or Berg. NASA's March 2026 slides cut mission science teams from "18 Mission Teams to 10 DART Teams" but do not name PMM or X-CAL among the cuts ([NASA ESD, 2026](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/a175298a-2c27-49b1-991f-41000e915647)). *Moderate.*
- **Current versions.** The current L1C ATBD is **v1.11 (August 2026)**, written for V08. Its revision history runs from v1.6 (4/2016) through v1.9 (4/2022, the V07 document), v1.10 (02/2026) and v1.11 (08/2026, adding MWI and AMSR3) ([L1C ATBD v1.11](https://arthurhou.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/Documents/L1C_ATBD_GPM_V08.pdf); [v1.9](https://arthurhou.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/Documents/L1C_ATBD_v1.9_GPMV07.pdf)). Both pt2's "v1.6" and pt3's "v1.9" are superseded.
  - "No changes were made to the GMI brightness temperature."
  - Each other sensor has its own static offset table, of different vintages: XCAL2016 (AMSR2, SAPHIR, MetOp-B MHS), XCAL2019 (MetOp-C MHS, NOAA-20 ATMS), XCAL2021 (SSMIS, TMI), XCAL2023 (NOAA-21 ATMS) and XCAL2026 (WSF-M1 MWI, AMSR3). *High.*
- **Gauge calibration of the Late Run.** Early and Late are calibrated to the Final Run "with climatological coefficients that vary by month and location". The coefficients are computed after the Final Run is reprocessed "when a new version of IMERG is released" ([IMERG V07 ATBD, 13 Aug 2026](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IMERG_V07_ATBD_final_260813.pdf)). For V07 they were computed once, after the V07B Final Run, and in use from 1 June 2024 ([V07 release notes, 2024](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/IMERG_V07_ReleaseNotes_241126.pdf)).
  - The August 2026 ATBD still names GPCC **V2022**.
  - No document says the coefficients were frozen or updated after the Final record ended on 2025-09-30. By the method described, they have not needed updating: they are a fixed climatology.
  - GPCC V2025 "merges the former Full Data Monthly with the Monitoring Product" ([DWD/GPCC, 2025](https://opendata.dwd.de/climate_environment/GPCC/html/gpcc_precipitation_analysis_monthly_v2025_doi_download.html), read through an extract; [ICDC, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.geo.uni-hamburg.de/en/service/icdc/about-icdc/news/2026/20260327-new-verison-of-gpcc-product.html)). IMERG's documentation says that when GPCC updates, "we switch to the new version and continue processing forward" ([IMERG Technical Documentation, 2023](https://arthurhou.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/Documents/IMERG_TechnicalDocumentation_final.pdf)).
  - **Inference, not a NASA statement:** V2025 can reach the Late Run only through a V08 Final Run and new climatological coefficients.
  - The V08 transition page covers only the separate GPROF-to-CORRA climatology ([GPM, 28 Apr 2026](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/imerg-v08-transition-schedule)). *Moderate.*
- **If GMI or DPR stopped.** No document read names a backup reference.
  - The ATBD says GMI calibrates all other microwave sensors (TMI did this in the TRMM era). The system "can run with the then-current calibration files" through dropouts, and such dropouts "are rare for the GMI and DPR".
  - It also says a drifting GMI "cannot be accommodated", and that changing this "would require a major development effort" ([ATBD 2026](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IMERG_V07_ATBD_final_260813.pdf)).
  - PPS's anomaly table logs GMI electronics side switches on 25 Mar and 24 Jun 2026, and a reaction-wheel anomaly on 8 Sep 2026 ([PPS, rev. 25 Sep 2026](https://gpmweb2https.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/tsdis/AB/docs/gpm_anomalous.html)).
  - No GPROF V08 ATBD was found. *Moderate.*
- **MetOp-C.** PPS's 20 Nov 2026 notice says "METOPC has incorrectly used METOPB calibration files" in V08A and V08B. Production processing has stopped and the data will be reprocessed, but "NRT continues its processing METOPC GPROF data" ([PPS, 20 Nov 2026](https://pps.gsfc.nasa.gov/Documents/PPS_Will_Reprocess_All_METOPC_GPROF_Data.txt)). **Whether the near-real-time MetOp-C GPROF feeding the Late Run has the same error is not stated.** *High that it is unstated.*

## Inputs now

- **The near-real-time stream after the losses: not published.** PPS's news table has no notice on SSMIS after 30 Sep 2026, on NOAA's AMSR2 NRT feed, or on S-NPP ATMS ([PPS home, 24 Nov 2026](https://pps.gsfc.nasa.gov/)). PPS's per-sensor empty-granule logs end in 2024 (SSMIS, ATMS) and 2020 (AMSR2) ([PPS logs](https://gpmweb2https.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/tsdis/AB/docs/partner_anomalous.html)). Its near-real-time page lists products, not sensors ([GPM, PPS NRT](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/sources/pps-nrt)). Only the Late Run's file headers or the jsimpson listings, both behind a PPS sign-up, would show it. *Moderate.*
- **AMSR3.** It is in PPS production: L1C from 25 Aug 2026, and standard and climate GPROF V08B released 4 Nov 2026 ([PPS, 29 Oct 2026](https://pps.gsfc.nasa.gov/Documents/L2_GPROF_V08B_Data_Release_New_WSFM1_MWI_L1_L2_and_GOSAT_GW_AMSR3_L2_L3%20Products.txt)). NOAA moved its AMSR products, L1B and precipitation included, to AMSR3 when AMSR2 NRT ended ([NOAA OSPO, 19 Nov 2026](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/11/MSG_20261119_1548.html)). **No source says the Late Run uses AMSR3.** *Moderate.*
- **JMA forecast.** Phase (PLP) comes from "JMA Forecast" for both Early and Late. PPS gets that forecast from JAXA, and the Early and Late GPROF retrievals also use JMA output as ancillary data ([Technical Documentation](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-07/IMERG_TechnicalDocumentation_final_230713.pdf); [ATBD](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IMERG_V07_ATBD_final_260813.pdf)). No 2026 notice on this feed was found. *High.*
- **Autosnow.** Early and Late have used the 2-km Autosnow since 2 July 2023. NOAA upgraded Autosnow on 19 Aug 2026 ([NOAA OSPO, 2026](https://ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/08/MSG_20260819_1630.html)). PPS then found its standard GPROF V08 preprocessor "neglecting to use any new v1r1 AUTOSNOW data that includes AMSR3" ([PPS, 18 Sep 2026](https://pps.gsfc.nasa.gov/Documents/PPS_Temporarily_Halting_GPM_V08_STD_L2_L3_GPROF.txt)). **Which version the V07 Late Run reads is not stated.** *Moderate.*
- **GEOS FP did not end.** On 24 Nov 2026 GMAO's status page shows "GEOS-FP 5.44 | Nominal" and GEOS-IT running ([GMAO status](https://gmao.gsfc.nasa.gov/operations/status.php)). An upgrade to 5.44.6 was set for 18 Nov 2026 ([GMAO, 2026](https://gmao.gsfc.nasa.gov/geos-system-news/aerosol-updates-in-the-geos-fp-upgrade-to-geos-5445/)).
  - GEOS-IT replaces FP-IT ([GMAO](https://gmao.gsfc.nasa.gov/gmao-products/geos-it/)).
  - The 31 May 2026 end, known only from a search summary of an Earthdata Forum post that now returns 404, concerns a "GMAO-FP" ancillary stream or FP-IT, not GEOS FP itself. *High.*
- **Meteosat-12 in CPC's merged IR: not shown.** The CMR record lists "METEOSAT-5/7/8/9/10/11" ([CMR GPM_MERGIR](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/collections.umm_json?short_name=GPM_MERGIR)). GES DISC's list of 17 Apr 2025 names Meteosat-11 at 9.5°E, yet its 2025 table row shows MSG-3 (Meteosat-10) ([GES DISC, 2025](https://docserver.gesdisc.eosdis.nasa.gov/public/project/GPM/CPC-4kmIR-Sats.pdf)). CPC's own README is stale ([CPC](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/global_precip/html/README)). This is unresolved; the file metadata would settle it. *Low.*
  - The same CMR record says the geo-IR is "dynamically calibrated to GOES East, using a 35 day trailing inter-calibration". That is one more calibration dependency, on NOAA's GOES-East.
- **Latency figures differ, even within NASA's own documents.** For the Late Run they give:
  - 12 hours in Table 3 of the 2026 ATBD, and about 14 h in its text ([ATBD](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IMERG_V07_ATBD_final_260813.pdf));
  - "almost 13 hr" in the Technical Documentation;
  - 14–15 h in README.GIS ([PPS, 2023](https://arthurhou.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/Documents/README.GIS.pdf)).

  This is unresolved and is probably a target figure against a typical one; this answer relies on none of them.

## Alternatives

- **For the inputs.** IMERG's documents name no real-time substitute for CPC's merged IR. GridSat is mentioned only for the gap before 2000, and using it "would require study" ([ATBD](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IMERG_V07_ATBD_final_260813.pdf)). For GEOS FP, PPS "just uses the most recent forecast sequence", and forecasts a few days old still work ([Technical Documentation](https://arthurhou.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/Documents/IMERG_TechnicalDocumentation_final.pdf)). Outside model substitutes (JMA GSM, GFS, ECMWF) were not documented by NASA and not searched. *Moderate.*
- **For the dataset**, as of Nov 2026:

## Copies

- **PPS retention.** PPS keeps Early and Late HDF5 and GIS files "until a new version of the Early and Late IMERG algorithm replaces them", which it says gives over 20 years of record. It serves the Late Run from jsimpsonhttps and the Final Run from arthurhouhttps ([PPS README.GIS, 2023](https://arthurhou.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/Documents/README.GIS.pdf)). No 2026 statement was found; the expected switch to V08 is when replacement would happen. *Moderate.*
- **Other copies: none current found.**
  - NASA's catalog lists only GES DISC, cloud-hosted, for GPM_3IMERGHHL and GPM_3IMERGDL ([CMR](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/collections.json?keyword=IMERG%20Late&page_size=50)).
  - NCAR GDEX holds the V07 *Final* Run only, to 2025-10-01 ([GDEX d731000](https://gdex.ucar.edu/datasets/d731000/)).
  - The IRI Data Library held a V06 IMERG_Late copy. Its full shutdown was expected "by the end of October 2026" ([IRI](https://iri.columbia.edu/resources/data-library/sunset/)), and the catalog now requires sign-in ([IRIDL](https://iridl.ldeo.columbia.edu/SOURCES/.NASA/.GES_DISC/.GPM_L3/index.html)).
  - Worldview's tutorial does not say which run its IMERG layer uses ([Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/tutorials/view-imerg-precipitation-imagery-worldview)).
  - Hosts outside the allowed domains (AWS, Google, Earth Engine, dynamical.org) were out of scope. *Moderate.*

## Ratings against the criteria

## Gaps

- **Not published:**
  - what PPS's near-real-time stream ingests now;
  - whether the Late Run uses AMSR3, which Autosnow version it reads, and whether its near-real-time MetOp-C has the calibration-file error;
  - whether the Late coefficients changed after 2025-09-30;
  - Meteosat-12 in GPM_MERGIR.
- **Who holds the answers:**
  - PPS (the Late Run file headers on jsimpson, behind a free PPS sign-up; or the PPS help desk) for all but the last;
  - GES DISC (GPM_MERGIR file metadata) for Meteosat-12.
- **X-CAL funding:** not found. The PMM Science Team award records (NSPIRES) or Kroodsma's branch would hold it.
- **GSMaP status:** JAXA's pages couldn't be read. The sandbox refused www.eorc.jaxa.jp, and sharaku returned 404. One deferred call, a Wayback fetch of JAXA's handbook, is in `needs-approval.md`.
- **Checks:** the Checker had only about 3 minutes and confirmed the passages it reached for the GEOS FP, MetOp-C and GPCC claims. All other passages were checked against the saved texts by script only. The Reconciler added the latency conflict and the GOES-East calibration point.
- `check-by-hand.md` lists the unread pages. `sites-to-trust.md` lists 5 hosts the sandbox refused; you can decide whether to add any to the allowed domains.
- **RRS-1:** the geographic independence of PPS and GES DISC (Earthdata Cloud) was not re-checked here; see the pt3 run.
