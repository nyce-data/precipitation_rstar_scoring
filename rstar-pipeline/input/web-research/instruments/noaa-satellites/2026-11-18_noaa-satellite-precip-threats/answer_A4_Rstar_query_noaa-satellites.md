# Is NOAA's satellite precipitation observing at risk? (Jan 2025 – Nov 18, 2026)

**Yes, and part of it has already happened.** Since January 2025 NOAA has lost several microwave inputs to its precipitation products: the legacy POES satellites (data ended June 16, 2025), Suomi NPP (data ended November 2, 2026) and JAXA's AMSR2 near-real-time feed (ended September 2026). DoD's DMSP microwave data has been "data of opportunity" since mid-2025, and whether it still reaches NOAA after September 30, 2026 is unconfirmed. The main forward risk is a projected gap of a few months with no geostationary spare (October 2032 to early 2033), found by both GAO and the Commerce Inspector General, after GeoXO was restructured and cut to four satellites. Budget cuts are proposed but so far blunted by Congress. NOAA's core sources today (GOES-18/19, NOAA-20/21, Metop-B/C, GPM) are working. **Overall confidence: moderate to high.** The main open question is DMSP delivery after September 2026.

## Key findings

**Products already run on fewer microwave satellites.** OSPO's retirement table shows the NOAA-19 and DMSP F17/F18 inputs retired from Blended Rain Rate and TPW on January 30, 2025 and from MiRS and eTRaP on April 8, 2025, with SNPP, NOAA-20/21, Metop-B/C, GPM and GCOM-W as the mitigation ([OSPO](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/products/archive-retirements.html)). The current Blended Rain Rate page lists seven satellites ([OSPO](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/products/bRR/)). Two of those seven have since gone: Suomi NPP data ended "Permanent[ly]" on November 2, 2026 ([NESDIS, Aug 3, 2026](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/cessation-of-suomi-national-polar-orbiting-partnership-s-npp-data-users-onafter-november-2-2026); [Update #4](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/update-4-cessation-of-suomi-national-polar-orbiting-partnership-s-npp-data-users-november-02-2026-1540-utc-and-decommissioning-issued-november-2-2026-1800-utc)), and the AMSR2 near-real-time feed ended in September 2026 ([OSPO, Jul 28, 2026](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/07/MSG_20260728_1045.html)). In 2023 NESDIS warned that without DMSP, NOAA-19 and S-NPP microwave data "there would be significant degradations in precipitation products for several hours each day" ([NESDIS SAT memo](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/sat/recommendationLibrary/SAT_Memo_Impacts_of_Legacy_Sensors_non-NWP_20230615_Final.pdf)). No document read measures the degradation actually seen. **Confidence: high** for the losses, **low** for their effect.

**DMSP is the most fragile partner feed.** FNMOC reversed its 2025 cutoff: "there will be no interruption to DMSP data delivery", but on a "best effort (8x5) basis" ([OSPO, Jul 30, 2025](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2025/07/MSG_20250730_0015.html)). DoD would share SSMIS data until the sensor fails or the program ends, "currently planned for September 2026" ([NASA GPM](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/ssmis-data-collection-continue-2026); [NSIDC](https://nsidc.org/data/user-resources/data-announcements/user-notice-ssmis-processing-will-now-continue-through-september-2026)). On October 13, 2026 three senators cited "the U.S. Space Force's plan to sustain operations of the three remaining DMSP spacecraft through their functional end of life, currently estimated no earlier than 2028". They also asked whether DoD will continue to share the data with NOAA, and warned that DMSP's retirement leaves the early-morning orbit "without dedicated U.S. microwave coverage" ([Hickenlooper, Murkowski, King, 2026](https://www.hickenlooper.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2026-DMSP-Continuity-Letter-Hickenlooper-Murkowski-King.pdf)). **Confidence: moderate.**

**The geostationary risk is a gap in the spare, not a loss of rain coverage today.** GAO: "the current operational spare satellite (GOES-16) is projected to meet the end of its life in October 2032", while GeoXO-1 is not operational until early 2033 ([GAO-26-107739](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107739/index.html)). The OIG adds that GOES-17's life ends by 2030, and that the likelihood of a continuity gap "continues to increase" ([OIG-26-009-I](https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/OIG-26-009-I_FinalReport_SECURED.pdf)). GAO concludes it is "unclear whether GeoXO will improve, or even continue, the functionality available in the current generation". GeoXO was restructured after OMB's April 2025 passback directed NESDIS to "immediately cancel all major instrument and spacecraft" contracts and to cut lifecycle costs by 50 percent with outyear costs below $500M ([OMB passback, via KTOO](https://media.ktoo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/noaa-passback2025.pdf); [CRS R49026](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-07-10_R49026_6562c219cbc284424293d617f272b0abd3a13ddf.html)). GAO adds that GeoXO got an extra $113 million in FY2026 to speed up the sounder, and that Commerce must send Congress a baseline report by December 31, 2026. On the GOES rain products themselves, NESDIS retired the legacy GOES-R Rainfall Rate/QPE on November 2, 2026, saying the Enterprise product "fully covers" it ([OSPO](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/10/MSG_20261030_1330.html)). **Confidence: high.**

**Budget cuts to the precipitation-relevant lines are proposed, not enacted.** The FY2027 request cuts Polar Weather Satellites to $269.6M (−$20.4M, with JPSS-3 put into long-term storage). It cuts OSPO operations to $225.0M (−$47.8M, through automation and a contractor-operated model "beginning with GOES-R") and STAR product development to $43.5M (−$16.3M). With that cut, the share of Enterprise algorithms supported falls to 85% instead of 95% ([NOAA FY2027 CJ](https://www.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/FY2027-NOAA-CJ-Submission.pdf)). Congress enacted FY2026 PWS at $290.0M and GEO at $497.9M ([FY2026 JES](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fy26_cjs_jes.pdf)). The House FY2027 mark gives GEO $677.0M ([H. Rept. 119-652](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-119hrpt652/html/CRPT-119hrpt652.htm)), and the Senate committee mark gives the same and urges avoiding descoping to prevent coverage gaps ([Senate statement](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/cjs_explanatory_stmt.pdf)). The CR runs to December 11, 2026 and lets NOAA spend fast enough to keep GeoXO launch schedules; without a new law or CR, a funding gap begins December 12, 2026 ([CRS R49353](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-17_R49353_98ddd8e7b1852b47b24e394ed7ddf6d6d53b4632.html)). **Confidence: high** for the figures.

## Conflicting evidence

- **DMSP after September 30, 2026 (unresolved).** OSPO's September 1, 2026 notice moves F-16/17/18 SSMI/S files to direct delivery to NCCF from October 1, 2026 ([OSPO](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/09/MSG_20260901_1930.html)), and the senators report a Space Force plan to operate DMSP to 2028 or later. Against that, NSIDC, NCEI and NASA gave September 2026 as the mission end. A CLASS notice of October 23, 2026 reportedly says the data flow stopped on 9/30/2026, but it was seen only as a search summary and could not be opened. An OSPO notice also reports DMSP processing stopped from June 19, 2026, with the end "TBD" (checker; no resolution notice found).
- **GeoXO lifecycle cost.** NOAA's FY2027 CJ totals $11.94B; GAO reports about $13B for the April 2026 baseline. The sources don't explain the difference; the CJ may predate the April 2026 rebaseline.
- **AMSR2 to AMSR3.** OSPO says the AMSR precipitation type/rate product moves to AMSR3 "seamlessly without any gap" (September 2, 2026), but the blended products lose AMSR2 with no date for adding AMSR3. Whether the transition happened on schedule was not found.
- **Times of day.** Suomi NPP cessation was planned for 13:00 UTC (August 3 notice) but took effect at 15:40 UTC (Update #4). NOAA-19's decommissioning is 16:55 UTC in the NESDIS notice and 18:55 UTC on the OSPO POES status page. Neither changes the dates.
- **JPSS-4 date.** The CJ's launch commitment of Q1 FY2028 and GAO's launch readiness in 2027 measure different things and are consistent.

## Gaps

- DMSP delivery after September 30, 2026: held by NOAA OSPO/CLASS and Navy FNMOC. The CLASS notice is listed in `check-by-hand.md` (class.noaa.gov refused, and no archived copy after May 2026). The senators asked DoD and NOAA for a written reply within 30 days of October 13, 2026.
- GPM: NASA's 2026 Earth Science Senior Review guidance letters (August 2026) are not posted on its review page. A CEOS database snippet gives a GPM end of life of September 2029 (not read).
- NOAA-20/21 design life (seven years) comes from search summaries only; the NESDIS page refused scripts, the Wayback Machine and the browser.
- Staffing: no NESDIS, STAR or OSPO staffing cut figures were found. The FY2027 CJ shows no position change on the OSPO and STAR cuts.
- Executive Order 14369 (December 18, 2025) and the 2026 NOAA reorganization were found only in summaries. FY2025 NESDIS line amounts were never published (full-year CR).
