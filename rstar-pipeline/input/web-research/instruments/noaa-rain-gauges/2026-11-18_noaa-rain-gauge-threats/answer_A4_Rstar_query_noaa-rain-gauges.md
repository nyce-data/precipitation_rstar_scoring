# Is NOAA's ground-based precipitation network at risk? (January 2025 – November 18, 2026)

**Yes, in part. Confidence: moderate to high.**
- **ASOS (imminent):** the threat is already in effect, and it comes from operations and budgets, not from cuts to NOAA's own observing budget:
  - FAA money is short, so 206–207 FAA-owned ASOS sites are being converted to AWOS-C between 2025 and 2029;
  - technician shortages since 2025 have stretched maintenance;
  - at least one long ASOS outage has already ended an official climate record (Frankfort, KY).
- **COOP (emerging):** NWS has formally proposed to "right-size" the ~6,700-site network and may lean on mesonet or contractor data, on aging equipment its maker no longer supports. Nothing has been decided yet.
- **USCRN (emerging, rejected so far):** both budget requests would close the lab that maintains it, but Congress rejected this for FY2026 and the network is running normally.
- **CoCoRaHS:** no threat was found.

Nothing shows a precipitation network being defunded or terminated. NWS's observing budget has stayed roughly flat, and Congress has turned down every cut proposed so far.

## Key findings

**Budget: flat for gauges, with cuts proposed elsewhere (high confidence).**
- NWS's operations line for Observations, which covers ASOS, COOP, NEXRAD, buoys and radiosondes:
  - $266.8M actual FY2025;
  - $262.5M enacted FY2026;
  - $262.5M requested for FY2027 ([NOAA FY2027 CJ](https://www.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/FY2027-NOAA-CJ-Submission.pdf); [FY2026 joint explanatory statement](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fy26_cjs_jes.pdf)).
- The FY2027 request's two cuts are to weather buoys and the Voluntary Observing Ship program (−$1.8M) and to NEXRAD radome upkeep (−$1.5M). Neither touches the gauges.
- The procurement line that pays for equipment refresh dropped from $36.2M (FY2025 actual) to $16.2M (FY2026 enacted). NOAA's FY2027 request adds $2.0M and says "Over half of NWS major observing systems and capabilities are beyond or reaching end-of-life" ([FY2027 CJ](https://www.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/FY2027-NOAA-CJ-Submission.pdf)).
- Congress added at least $10M for full forecast-office staffing in FY2026 ([joint explanatory statement](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fy26_cjs_jes.pdf)).
- NOAA has run on a CR at FY2026 rates since Oct 1, 2026, ending Dec 11, 2026 (P.L. 119-103). The CR has no NWS-specific provision.

**COOP: restructuring proposed, on unsupported equipment (high confidence for the proposal; low for any closures).**
- NWS's May 7, 2026 Federal Register notice lists "Right-sizing and optimizing the scope and coverage of the existing ~6,700-site COOP network". It asks when mesonet data would be "suitable alternatives to federally managed COOP stations" and whether volunteers should move from manual observing to "site stewardship" ([Federal Register 2026-09099](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-09099.pdf)).
- A June 12, 2026 sources-sought notice weighs three models across about 6,700 sites: government-run, contractor-run, and buying data as a service from mesonets. It seeks pricing for up to 3,000 all-in-one units. This was read only through an aggregator's copy ([Govly](https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16692860)).
- Both CJs say COOP "relies on equipment that is no longer supported by manufacturers", yet they only plan to "initiate refresh" ([FY2026 CJ](https://www.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/NOAA%20FY26%20Congressional%20Justification.pdf); [FY2027 CJ](https://www.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/FY2027-NOAA-CJ-Submission.pdf)).
- No 2025–2026 station closures, observer losses or COOP staff losses were documented.
- The ~6,700 figure is well below the 8,500–12,000 that older NWS and NCEI pages give. No source explains the difference (unresolved).

**ASOS: conversion and maintenance strain are happening now (high confidence on conversion; moderate on staffing).**
- The NWS FAQ says "The FAA funding budgeted for this upgrade is insufficient to recondition all 570 FAA ASOS locations." It moves 207 sites to AWOS-C on a schedule from 2025 through 2029 ([NWS ASOS FAQ](https://www.weather.gov/asos/faq.html)). FAA's own page says 206 ([FAA ASWON](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/weather/aswon)); neither source explains the difference.
- FAA says AWOS-C reports precipitation accumulation, but NWS says an AWOS "cannot serve as a direct replacement for official NWS climate archiving." It said this when it retired the Frankfort, KY ASOS as a climate site; that ASOS had been "offline since late June 2025" ([NWS Louisville, July 17, 2026](https://www.weather.gov/media/lmk/2026/KFFT%20Press%20Release.docx.pdf)).
- How many of the 207 sites are climate stations was not found.
- Staffing:
  - A March 20, 2025 ASOS program memo reorganized maintenance into "pods" "to mitigate critical staffing shortages" ([NWS ASOS Current Events](https://www.weather.gov/asos/CurrentEvents.html)).
  - The Commerce OIG counted 564 NWS departures, about 13% of staff, between January and April 2025 ([OIG Top Management Challenges FY2026](https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/OIG-26-003_TMC_FY_2026_SECURED-1.pdf)).
  - A NOAA staffer told CNN there were "more than 90 vacancies among the staff responsible for repairing NWS Doppler radars and automated surface weather observation stations" ([CNN, May 2, 2025](https://www.cnn.com/2025/05/02/weather/nws-forecasting-layoffs-trump), read via saved copy).
  - Hiring under a 450-position authority had reached more than 300 by mid-2026 (CBS, E&E). No count of electronics technician vacancies after rehiring was found.
- Site-level gauge outages are documented at Springfield IL, Augusta ME, Houston IAH (rain gauge damaged around Nov 24, 2025) and others. No national outage figure is published.
- Against this:
  - all 1,529 ASOS 2.0 processor units were delivered by 2025;
  - the CJs fund "new sensors to improve precipitation estimates";
  - Senate appropriators back extending ASOS service life;
  - S. 3923, passed by the Senate on Nov 10, 2026 and pending in the House, would require an ASOS assessment and a GAO report.

**USCRN: proposed closure of its maintainer, rejected so far (high confidence).**
- Both requests say NOAA "will close … the Air Resources Laboratory (ARL) in College Park, MD, Idaho Falls, ID, and Oak Ridge, TN, as well as a nation-wide network of soil moisture sensors". This is part of ending OAR's Climate Labs ([FY2027 CJ](https://www.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/FY2027-NOAA-CJ-Submission.pdf)).
- The FY2026 request would have cut Climate Labs from 216 positions and 204 FTE to zero. Congress instead funded Climate Labs at $104.1M in FY2026.
- ARL finished a transmitter upgrade on 139 towers in 45 states on April 25, 2026 ([ARL](https://www.arl.noaa.gov/news-pubs/arl-news-stories/upgrading-arls-largest-meteorological-network/)).
- A 2025 NOAA report assessed downsizing USCRN's soil-moisture sensors (not its gauges).
- The FY2027 outcome depends on the bill that follows the CR.

**CoCoRaHS and other networks.**
- **CoCoRaHS:** no cut was found (moderate confidence: absence of evidence, and its current NOAA funding is not public).
- **Mesonet:** the FY2026 request "to eliminate approximately 1,200 observation platforms" was rejected ([H. Rept. 119-272](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-119hrpt272/html/CRPT-119hrpt272.htm)). Because the COOP plan leans on mesonets, that budget line now matters more to gauge coverage.
- **SNOTEL, NADP and RCCs (USDA, USGS and NOAA respectively):** 2025 disruptions are documented in news, letters and minutes (moderate confidence).

## What losing gauges would mean, and how far radar and satellites can fill in

**Flood forecasting.**
- MRMS's gauge-corrected radar estimate "has proven to outperform the Radar-Only product." Where gauges are sparse, it falls back on radar ([NWS WDTD MRMS guide](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/wdtd/-/qpe-w-gauge-bias-correcti-1)).
- In the 2013 loss of 63 hourly gauges in the Susquehanna basin, most crest forecasts moved less than 10%. But 9% were degraded by more than 20%, mainly in high-impact events ([MARFC study, AMS 2014](https://ams.confex.com/ams/94Annual/webprogram/Manuscript/Paper241627/FINAL%20-%20Retrospective%20Case%20Study%20of%20the%20Impact%20of%20Rain%20Gage%20Network%20Reductions%20on%20National%20Weather%20Service%20River%20Forecasts%20in%20the%20Susquehanna%20River%20Basin.pdf)).

**Radar gaps.** Radar misses or underestimates rain where the beam is blocked or overshoots ([NRC, 2005](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11128/chapter/7)). An NWS study found it covered under half of the Northwest RFC area in the cool season (an older study).

**Satellite.** IMERG underestimates heavy rain (>10 mm/h) over the U.S. and is itself gauge-adjusted ([IMERG evaluation, 2022](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/47949/noaa_47949_DS1.pdf)).

**Drought monitoring and climate records.**
- COOP and CoCoRaHS feed the U.S. Drought Monitor ([NDMC slides, 2022](https://media.cocorahs.org/docs/WERA1012_2022_NDMC.pdf)).
- GHCN-Daily is the official archive for COOP data ([NCEI GHCNd](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/global-historical-climatology-network-daily)).
- Converted or closed sites break long records, as Frankfort shows.

Radar and satellite can fill spatial gaps for real-time warning. They cannot replace gauges for bias correction, snow, terrain or the long, uniform records that climate normals need. (This is my interpretation of the sources above, not a quoted finding.)

## Gaps and uncertainties

- **COOP modernization outcome:** no decision, site list or award has been published. NWS holds them; watch SAM.gov 1305M2-COOP and weather.gov/coop.
- **COOP size:** ~6,700 sites in 2026 against older counts of 8,500–12,000; NWS has not explained the difference.
- **ASOS:** which of the 207 AWOS-C sites are climate stations (NWS's linked site list was not read). No national ASOS outage or technician-vacancy count is public; the OIG staffing audit 2025-523 has not been published.
- **USCRN:** its budget line is not identified in any budget document. The House FY2027 figure for OAR Climate Labs was not checked.
- **CoCoRaHS:** its current NOAA funding route and amount are not public; CSU or NOAA would hold them.
- **FAA:** the FY2027 Weather Systems line was not read; its budget PDF was refused.
- **Not researched (time and token limits):** USGS gauges, HADS, IFLOWS and ALERT flood gauges, and the DuckDuckGo second-engine pass. The budget, not the evidence, ended the research.
- **Pages to check by hand:** `check-by-hand.md` lists 17 pages that could not be read.
- **Sandbox:** it refused 8 hosts. They are listed in `sites-to-trust.md` for the user to decide whether to allow them.
