**Yes. Several of the Late Run's microwave inputs have already been lost or disrupted, but none of the losses stops production. Each one leaves fewer satellite passes, so the Late Run leans more on infrared and morphing.**

- **Already gone:** MHS on NOAA-18 and NOAA-19 (2025) and ATMS on Suomi NPP (2 Nov 2026).
- **Reached its end date:** the DMSP SSMIS program (F16/F17/F18), about a quarter of IMERG's microwave data, reached its formal end in Sept 2026, and its feed broke on 30 Sep 2026.
- **Feed ended:** NOAA's near-real-time AMSR2 feed ended in Sept 2026.
- **Largest remaining risk:** the GPM Core Observatory's funding, not its health.
- **What NASA has done:** brought in partial substitutes (WSF-M1 MWI since Aug 2026; AMSR3 in PPS production). It has announced no pause and no Late Run version change beyond V08, which is due no sooner than winter 2026.

Overall confidence: **moderate**. What happened at the providers is well documented. What PPS's near-real-time stream actually received after each loss is not documented anywhere I could read.

## NASA's response so far

- **Substitutes, not pauses.** PPS added WSF-M1 MWI and AMSR3. It announced no Late Run pause tied to an input loss, and I found no PPS or IMERG notice on S-NPP's end or on SSMIS after Sept 2026 ([GPM data news](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news), newest item 19 Aug 2026). **High** for what was announced; the near-real-time inputs themselves are unknown.
- **PPS's own V08 processing has had setbacks** (standard products, not near-real-time):
  - On 18 Sep 2026 PPS halted V08 GPROF and retracted data from 18–19 Aug for GMI, SSMIS, AMSR2, MHS and ATMS, because NOAA's new AUTOSNOW, which includes AMSR3, was being ignored.
  - On 1 Oct 2026 it recalled all V08 L3 GPROF for a flag bug.
  - On 20 Nov 2026 it began reprocessing all MetOp-C GPROF.
- **Late Run outages from PPS itself:** a production delay in June 2024, the IR outage of Oct 2024, and a planned near-real-time shutdown of about 14 h on 20 Jan 2026.
- **Version:** the Late Run stays on V07B in hybrid mode. Its switch to V08 is due no sooner than winter 2026 (Part 1; GPM's Aug 2026 schedule).

## How it adds up

The pattern is gradual thinning of the low-orbit microwave constellation rather than one decisive cut. Three sensor groups are gone or past their end dates within 17 months: NOAA-18/19 MHS, S-NPP ATMS and DMSP SSMIS. Political or budget decisions are tied to an input only in two cases:
- DoD's 2025 decision on DMSP, which sources tie directly to SSMIS;
- NOAA's "natural aging" end of POES.

The SSMIS loss weighs most. GPM's own estimate more than doubles the Late Run's reliance on IR without it. MWI offsets part of that loss.

## Gaps and uncertainties

- **What PPS's near-real-time system ingested** after 30 Sep 2026 (SSMIS), 2 Sep 2026 (AMSR2) and 2 Nov 2026 (S-NPP) is not published. PPS holds it: the input lists in IMERG Late file headers, or its near-real-time pages, need a free PPS sign-up.
- **JAXA's own GCOM-W/AMSR2 notices** couldn't be read: the sandbox refused JAXA's hosts and the browser was rejected. A JAXA snippet refers to a "GCOM-W operational change" on 2 Sep 2026. Look at eorc.jaxa.jp's AMSR news.
- **EUMETSAT's pages** couldn't be read (the browser got a 403 too): the Indian Ocean coverage end date, Metop-B's end of life and the MetOp-SG news.
- **GEOS FP:** the Earthdata Forum thread was down (404).
- **Checker:** its OSPO search summaries were not opened. They cover WSF-M outages, the retirement of MetOp HRPT direct readout on 1 Sep 2026 (the global feeds continue), and the IODC safe mode on 15–16 Jul 2026.
- **Passages:** the checker checked 5 of 42 central passages word for word. I checked the rest against the saved files through the merge script.
