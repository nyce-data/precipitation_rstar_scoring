## Ratings by theme

1. **The IMERG science team's funding: emerging, possibly imminent (low confidence).** The [HQ slide](https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/documents/STM/2026-05/01_ceres-st-May2026-considine2.pdf) says mission science teams "including … PMM" are replaced by MIO "DART" teams. [NASA's PMM Science Team page](https://gpm.nasa.gov/science/pmm-science-team) says that team does "algorithm development … in support of TRMM and GPM". It also lists Huffman's IMERG work package. [NASA's MIO page](https://science.nasa.gov/earth-science/mio/) lists no GPM DART integrator. If the slide means that team, IMERG's algorithm maintenance loses its usual funding route. PPS's running of the Late Run is not named.
   - **Conflict (unresolved):** the IMERG team's own [2026 white paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260008217/downloads/PrecipWhitePaper_V4.pdf) and [GAO](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108556/index.html) use "PMM" for the planned *Precipitation Measuring Mission*, which hasn't flown. The slide doesn't say which it means. The white paper says MIO will have "Water" as a major theme.
   - No news covered this.
2. **Availability at GES DISC: imminent, already happening, but no data lost (high confidence).** The [DAAC consolidation](https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/documents/STM/2026-05/01_ceres-st-May2026-considine2.pdf) and the [cloud migration](https://github.com/nasa/gesdisc-tutorials/blob/main/user-guidance/GES_DISC_Migration_to_Earthdata_Website_and_Earthdata_Cloud.md) changed how the Late Run is reached:
   - on-premises HTTPS, OPeNDAP and THREDDS are gone, and scripts built on them stop working;
   - the Level 2 subsetter moved on Aug 5, 2026 and the L3/4 regridder-subsetter ends by Sep 15, 2026;
   - after Oct 1, 2026, all ingest interfaces are being converted to deliver straight to the cloud;
   - data stay "available for HTTPS download free of charge from the Earthdata Cloud".
   - No news covered it.
3. **GPM as a program: emerging (moderate confidence).** No outlet names GPM among the missions proposed for termination ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/white-house-budget-puts-54-nasa-science-missions-on-the-chopping-block/); [Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/articles/analyzing-the-fy-2027-nasa-budget-request)). The earlier funding run found that both requests give GPM $0.0 after FY2026, pending the 2026 Senior Review. The Senior Review's Earth science results are still not published (a DuckDuckGo search found only the call and the resources pages).
4. **Goddard staff and campus: imminent at center level, not tied to IMERG.** The cuts are enacted and already happening: about 22% of NASA's civil servants and 34% at Goddard were gone by January 2026 ([GAO](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108556/index.html); [NPR](https://www.kpbs.org/news/science-technology/2026/09/15/nasa-rushed-engineers-out-of-their-labs-they-say-it-risks-future-space-missions); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/nasa-goddards-shrinking-workforce-could-stifle-future-space-missions-experts-say/)). GAO adds that NASA officials said the FY26 request level "would have corresponded to a 32 percent reduction" in civil servants. SSAI filed two 2025 WARN notices that name no program ([Maryland DOL](https://dlr.maryland.gov/employment/warn.shtml)). No source says PPS, GES DISC or the IMERG team lost staff.
5. **The 2025 shutdown: no threat to the Late Run found.** The GPM site stopped updating its news pages ([Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20251020210650/https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news)). ESDIS metrics blame only an OMI-Aura delay on the shutdown ([ESDIS](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/about/data-metrics/esdis-metrics/weekly-metrics/10-20-2025)). The GPM data news has no outage item for Oct–Nov 2025. A search snippet (undated; the page itself was not opened) shows the same banner on the [IMERG Late Run Disasters Mapping Portal](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/directory/imerg-late-run-disasters-mapping-portal) page.
6. **Agency realignment and courts: no current threat found.** SMD was left unchanged, and NASA said "no reduction in force" ([Eos](https://eos.org/research-and-developments/nasa-announces-realignment-toward-human-spaceflight)). The court cases cover the Goddard library and collective bargaining ([D.D.C. filing](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.289587/gov.uscourts.dcd.289587.3.0.pdf); [Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/47041/)).
