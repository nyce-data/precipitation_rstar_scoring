GPCP Daily's calibration still rests on outside parties. No source says which SSMIS feed 1DD V1.3 uses now. The RSS SSMIS CDR it was built on ended on 2025-07-31, later than the 2023 date the question assumed, and FNMOC changed DMSP delivery on 1 October 2026. For V3.3, no funded reference after GPM is named. Other suppliers exist for the inputs and for the dataset as a whole, but there is no written plan for losing F17 or AIRS. Only V3.3 Daily has current copies off NASA's own systems: ESGF holds it at two US DOE sites and the CDS in Europe. Overall confidence: **moderate**. That supports DIRS-1, partly contradicts DIRS-5, and supports RRS-1 for V3.3 only.

## Calibration (q1)

- **SSMIS feed: not published (DIRS-1 supports; moderate).** The 1DD V1.3 code README (2017) names only "RSS SSMIS files" as its SSMIS input ([NCEI, GPCP Daily code package](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/sds/cdr/CDRs/Precipitation_GPCP-Daily/SourceCode_01B-35.tar.gz)). NCEI's record for the RSS V8 SSMIS CDR says it covers data "through July 31, 2025" and gives its status as "Complete" ([NCEI metadata C01567](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ncdc:C01567/html)). No GPCP, NCEI or GES DISC page found says what replaced it.
- **FNMOC, 1 October 2026 (DIRS-1 supports; high).** NOAA's notice says PolarProd ended, and FNMOC now sends DMSP xDR files "directly to NCCF" under new filenames. The F-17 and F-18 scan products in BUFR and HDF "will be discontinued" ([NOAA OSPO, 2026](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/09/MSG_20260901_1930.html)). One upstream path also carries all three SSMIS. In March 2026, F-17 was out from 21 March to 1426Z on 25 March, a 4-day outage traced to FNMOC; the notice prints "2025", a typo. F-16 and F-18 had outages too ([NOAA OSPO, Mar 2026](https://ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/03/MSG_20260326_1733.html)). The high-latitude AIRS/TOVS precipitation estimates are another outside service: the NASA/GSFC Sounder Research Team supplies them ([V3.3 Daily ATBD, last revised 12 Jun 2026](https://docserver.gesdisc.eosdis.nasa.gov/public/project/MEaSUREs/GPCP/GPCP_ATBD_V3.3_Daily.pdf)).
- **How long DMSP lasts: conflicting.** NSIDC passed on DoD's word that SSMIS processing continues "through the retirement of the DMSP mission, which is expected in September 2026", with gaps since spring 2025 ([NSIDC, 2025](https://nsidc.org/data/user-resources/data-announcements/user-notice-ssmis-processing-will-now-continue-through-september-2026)). A press report says the FY2026 NDAA keeps DMSP running "no earlier than 2028" ([Air & Space Forces, Aug 2026](https://airandspaceforces.com/space-force-legacy-weather-sats-2028-replacements)). FNMOC's October notice shows deliveries going on past September. No DoD page settles it.
- **Who keeps the intercalibration: not published for 1DD (DIRS-1 supports).** NASA PPS (X-CAL) has intercalibrated WSF-M1 MWI into 1C and GPROF NRT files since 12 Aug 2026 ([GPM, 2026](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/new-wsf-m1-mwi-nrt-files-available)). CSU says its SSM/I–SSMIS FCDR is continuing but names no version, funding or reference sensor ([CSU FCDR](https://rain.atmos.colostate.edu/FCDR/)).
- **After GPM: no named successor reference (DIRS-1 supports; moderate).**
  - GPM is one of 16 missions in the 2026 Senior Review (FY2027–29). Aqua's mission page posts its result, but no GPM outcome was found on the Senior Review page or elsewhere ([NASA call, 2025](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/earth-science-division/senior-reviews/2026/2026%20ESD%20Senior%20Review%20Call%20for%20Proposals_DRAFT20251219.pdf); [NASA Senior Review page](https://science.nasa.gov/earth-science/senior-review-2026/)).
  - Fuel lasts to December 2027 counting fuel alone, and solar activity moved the forecast end to "the late 2020's" ([NASA PMM summary, 2024](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/earth-science/summary-of-the-2023-precipitation-measurement-mission-science-team-meeting/); [NASA NTRS, 2024](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240004796)).
  - NASA wants GPM to overlap with AOS so the instruments can be cross-calibrated, and AOS will carry a JAXA Ku-band radar ([NASA NTRS, 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230007913)).
  - A GSFC white paper (preprint posted 27 Jul 2026) notes that "For nearly 30 years, the TRMM and GPM satellites served as references". It says JAXA's PMM "may be insufficient" as the next reference, because its radiometer lacks 19–37 GHz channels ([NASA GSFC, 2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260008217/downloads/PrecipWhitePaper_V4.pdf)).

## Alternatives (q2)

- **No written F17 or AIRS contingency (DIRS-5 partly contradicts; moderate).** The V3.3 Daily ATBD says days without TOVS or AIRS data are "set to missing" outside 55°N–S. It also records that Oct–Dec 2025 used IMERG Late, and Dec 2025 used GPROF V07, as stand-ins. Its V4 plan moves SSMIS to "WSF-M MWI" and replaces TOVS/AIRS with AVHRR ([GES DISC, V3.3 Daily ATBD](https://docserver.gesdisc.eosdis.nasa.gov/public/project/MEaSUREs/GPCP/GPCP_ATBD_V3.3_Daily.pdf)). Behrangi's 2026 slides say the team is switching to AVHRR and target V4 for mid-2027 ([Behrangi, IPWG 2026](https://ipwg12.imgw.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/S3.4-BEHRANGI-IPWG2026.pdf)). 1DD V1.3 has no such document.
- **The inputs today (high).**
  - AIRS was "healthy" on 30 Oct 2026. Since 4 Oct 2026 its L1B files have carried an advisory about telemetry out-of-limit flags caused by Aqua's orbital drift ([JPL AIRS report](https://airsnrt.jpl.nasa.gov/report/latest)).
  - That is past the 2023 projection of instrument shutdown on 26 Aug 2026 and passivation on 4 Nov 2026 ([NASA Aqua summary, 2023](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/aqua/AquaStatus_November.pdf)). The 2026 Senior Review recommended Aqua continue into FY27 ([NASA Aqua](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/aqua/)).
  - WSF-M01 data have been operational to NOAA through NCCF since 16 July 2026, and through PDA since 22 July 2026 ([NOAA OSPO, 2026](https://ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/07/MSG_20260720_1313.html)).
