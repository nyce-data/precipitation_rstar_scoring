**Short answer.** Only RRS-5 (survived a major disruption) is partly supported, and that support comes mostly from NCEI's own track record. RRS-2 (checksums quarterly or more often) and RRS-3 (disaster recovery plan tested in the past 2 years) find no public evidence either way. RRS-1 (geographically independent verified copies) is contradicted by every setup that is documented. RRS-4 (no imminent threat to funding) holds for now, but the threat is emerging. NCEI has the strongest credentials. It holds a current CoreTrustSeal certificate, valid to 2028-01-29. It kept its holdings through Hurricane Helene and three shutdowns, with "minimal" loss expected only for data that had not yet arrived. But no public document shows how often it checks fixity, a recent disaster recovery test, or a second copy far from Asheville. The AWS and BigQuery copies are single-region, provider-funded mirrors, and nothing shows they are checked against NCEI. **Overall confidence: moderate.** Several verdicts rest on searches that found nothing, and NCEI's CoreTrustSeal application, which likely answers RRS-2, could not be read.

## Integrity checks (RRS-2): no periodic fixity schedule found

- **GHCN-Daily's own maturity review** (DSMR-00227, 2021) scores Data Integrity at Level 2 of 5. It says "a checksum is stored in the archive catalog for each data file". That checksum "can be provided upon request", but none is put online with the data. The review also notes "No known audits performed on the archive at this time" (Preservability 4.5) ([DSMR-00227, Table 3](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/34330/noaa_34330_DS1.pdf)). **High** that this is what the review says.
- **NCEI's practice for providers** says its software "will validate the data files against their checksums" at submission and recommends SHA-2. No periodic re-check is described ([NCEI/IOOS submission practices](https://ioos.github.io/ncei-archiving-cookbook/practices.html)).
- **WDS-Paleo, an NCEI unit, in its 2020 CoreTrustSeal application:** MD5 is checked "any time a file is pulled". Monthly scripts compare file sizes, not checksums, between the primary and offsite copies. Fixity checks were still in the "implementation phase". This is the closest thing to a schedule found, and it falls short of RRS-2 ([WDS-Paleo CoreTrustSeal, 2020](https://www.coretrustseal.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/World-Data-Service-for-Paleoclimatology.pdf)).
- **Certification:** NCEI itself is on CoreTrustSeal's list of currently certified repositories, valid until **2028-01-29** (Requirements 2020–2022, published 2025-02-12). WDS-Paleo's certificate is valid until 2028-03-25. No ISO 16363 certification was found ([CoreTrustSeal certificates](https://amt.coretrustseal.org/certificates/)). **High.** The application's R7 and R14 answers would show NCEI's actual fixity practice, but they were not read (the sandbox refuses the host, and the browser hit a bot check).
- **NAO 212-15** requires "delivery to the archive and secure storage" but sets no checksum rule or frequency ([NAO 212-15](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/s3/2025-07/NAO-212-15.pdf)).
- **Cloud copies.** AWS says S3 "regularly verify the integrity of your data using checksums" and validates checksums on upload ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/DataDurability.html)). Google states no checksum practice for BigQuery and says "There is no service-level agreement (SLA) for the Public Dataset Program" ([Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/public-data)). No NOAA, NODD or provider document says either copy is verified against NCEI's. **Moderate.**

## Disaster recovery (RRS-3): required by policy, test results not public

- **The NESDIS policy** (NQP-3406) requires every system to have a contingency plan updated annually. "Testing/exercising must be conducted annually or more frequently", recorded on a Contingency Plan Test Results template, and a tabletop exercise alone is not enough for moderate- or high-impact systems ([NESDIS, 2015](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/s3/2021-08/NQP_Series_3406_NESDIS%20Contingency%20Planning%20Policy%20and%20Procedures_March_2015.pdf)). This is a requirement, not evidence that tests took place. WDS-Paleo's 2020 application says its recovery plan (ISCP) "is not publicly releasable".
- **OIG-24-034-A (Aug 2024)** found that the NCCF's moderate-impact cloud platform does not offer "testing contingency plans at alternate processing sites". It also found the NCCF "resides in a single region" despite a maximum tolerable downtime of "only a few hours", and despite the cloud provider's own multi-region recommendation for such systems. NOAA answered that it runs "in two separate cloud facilities within that region" ([OIG-24-034-A, p. 12](https://www.oig.doc.gov/OIGPublications/NOAANC_2.pdf)). Part 7 reported recommendations 3, 9 and 10 open in Sept 2025 ([Part 7](../2026-11-22_ghcn-daily-repository-pt7/answer.md)). **Oversight.gov now shows all three as "Closed"** ([Oversight.gov, rec 9](https://www.oversight.gov/reports/audit/lack-program-management-controls-and-attention-it-security-threatens-success-noaas-8)). Rec 9 is to move to "a FedRAMP approved high-impact cloud platform or provides the equivalent protection". The pages give no closure date or the action taken. OIG's March 2026 semiannual report had already dropped the report from its list of open recommendations ([OIG SAR, Mar 2026, Table 7-A](https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/SAR-2026-03.pdf); [Sept 2025](https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/SAR-2025-09.pdf)). **Moderate:** the closure is recorded, but its substance is not shown.
- **OIG-16-043-A (2016)** covers the October 2014 attack on NESDIS's satellite product system (ESPC), not NCEI. NESDIS "was unable to recover the satellite data gathered from October 20–22", and more than 16,000 firewall rules prolonged the restoration ([OIG-16-043-A](https://www.oig.doc.gov/OIGPublications/OIG-16-043-A.pdf)). It is a NESDIS data-loss precedent, but outside the archive. **High.**
- **Not found:** an NCEI continuity-of-operations or disaster recovery test date or result; a Helene after-action report; a GAO report on NOAA IT contingency planning (GAO's continuity reports cover satellites). OIG ended its audit of NWS radar continuity planning on 20 Aug 2025.

## Redundancy (RRS-1): copies exist, but not shown to be far apart or cross-checked

Both copies sit in the same metro area, so one regional disaster such as Helene could reach both. **Moderate:** this is the latest location found, and the cloud move may have changed it.
- **Boulder:** no document places a GHCN-D copy at Boulder. During Helene, NCEI used "facilities and staff in Colorado, Mississippi and Maryland" to restore ingest. That is spread-out operations, not a documented archive copy ([NCEI, 17 Oct 2024](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/node/6756)).

## Track record (RRS-5): the archive survived; some incoming data may not have

- **Hurricane Helene (27 Sep 2024).** Asheville lost power, water and communications, and the fire department supplied water to cool the servers. On 4 Oct, "All archived data at NCEI is currently inaccessible". By 29 Oct, all ingest streams were running and "more than 80% of the data" was recovered, and NOAA and NCEI expected "minimal data loss" ([NOAA, updated 29 Oct 2024](https://www.noaa.gov/news/some-noaa-ncei-websites-systems-down-due-to-helene-devastation-in-asheville-nc)). By 11 Dec, ingest was "fully restored" and 92% of the ingest backlog recovered, with help from Boulder ([NCEI FY25 Q1 State of the Services](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/FY2025%20Q1%20SOS%20Executive%20Summary.pdf)). NCEI had warned that "some observations might eventually be unrecoverable" ([NCEI, 17 Oct 2024](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/node/6756)), and an undated NCEI page says "a few datasets continue to be recovered". No final loss count was published, and nothing names GHCN-Daily. **Moderate.**
- **Shutdowns.** In Oct 2013 and Dec 2018–Jan 2019, ncdc.noaa.gov redirected to a lapse notice ([Wayback 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20131010090805/http://governmentshutdown.noaa.gov/); [2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20190110005407/https://governmentshutdown.noaa.gov/)). A NOAA spokesperson did not say why archived data were not left online ([EcoWatch, 2019](https://www.ecowatch.com/noaa-climate-data-offline-shutdown-2626963302.html)). The 2018 global analysis slipped to 6 Feb 2019 ([Sabin Center](https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/release-noaa-nasa-temperature-data-delayed)). In Oct 2025 the NCEI site "will not be updated" ([Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20251020000212/https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/)), yet `ghcnd_all.tar.gz` was rebuilt on 2025-10-18 mid-shutdown ([Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20251018190509/https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/ghcn/daily/)). About 95% of NCEI's Asheville staff were furloughed ([Citizen-Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/asheville-federal-workers-bare-minimum-090818131.html)). Commerce's 2019 lapse plan excepted 3 OCIO staff "to continue archiving and prevent loss or compromise of critical data" but did not name NCEI ([Commerce, 2019](https://www.commerce.gov/sites/default/files/2019-02/v8DOCLapsePlanOMBApprovedFebruary122019.pdf)). **No data loss from any shutdown was found** (moderate; an absence).
- **Current disruption.** The "Data Access Delays" alert (16–30 Nov 2026) blames the cloud migration and warns of "delayed data archival". It states no loss ([NCEI alerts](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/alerts)). Volume holds up: 3.6k TB delivered online in FY2026 Q1 ([NCEI SOS FY26 Q1](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/FY2026%20Q1%20SOS%20Executive%20Summary.pdf)).
