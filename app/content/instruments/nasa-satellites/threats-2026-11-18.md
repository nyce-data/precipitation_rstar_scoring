# Is NASA's satellite precipitation observing at risk? (Jan 2025 – Nov 18, 2026)

**Yes. The risk is mostly to what comes after GPM, and to the money and people behind it, not to today's data.** The GPM Core Observatory, the calibration anchor for IMERG, is healthy and in an extended mission. But NASA's FY2026 and FY2027 budget requests give it no funding of its own after FY2026, and the 2026 Senior Review that decides its FY2027–29 extension has published no result. Both requests would roughly halve Earth Science. Congress rejected that cut for FY2026 and has not yet decided FY2027 (the continuing resolution runs out December 11, 2026). The planned successors have been cut back. AOS was dropped and replaced by a smaller "FALCON" fleet. NASA's part in JAXA's PMM radar mission, which is GPM's successor, is now science expertise and a launch, with no fixed date. INCUS was proposed for closeout, though its satellites are still being built. TROPICS ended on November 12, 2025. Aqua is being closed out, but IMERG has not used Aqua data since 2011. Goddard has lost more than 40% of its workforce. PPS and GES DISC are still processing and distributing the data, while NASA plans to fold the data centers into thematic teams. **Overall confidence: moderate to high.** The open questions are the 2026 Senior Review decision and FY2027 appropriations.

## Key findings

**GPM's extension past FY2026 is undecided on paper.** NASA's 2023 decision extended GPM "through FY 2026 with additional funding above the baselined Senior Review budget target to support the near real-time processing system and two-reaction wheel science mode development" ([NASA, 2024](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/response-to-2023-earth-science-senior-review-and-2022-2023-out-of-cycle-senior-reviews-final.pdf)). The FY2026 request shows GPM at $19.7M for FY2026 and 0.0 for FY2027–30 ([NASA FY2026](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/fy-2026-budget-technical-supplement-002.pdf)). The FY2027 request also gives GPM 0.0 on its own line and says NASA "will allocate GPM funding beyond FY 2026 based on the 2026 Senior Review recommendations", from a pooled $68.8M Senior Review line ([NASA FY2027](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/fiscal-year-2027-full-budget-request.pdf)). The 2026 call invited GPM and Aqua, warning that "all missions proposing to the Senior Review will be given reduced budget targets" ([NASA call, Jan 16, 2026](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/earth-science-division/senior-reviews/2026/2026_ESD%20Senior_Review_Call_for_Proposals_FINAL.pdf)). A May 2026 NASA briefing expected guidance in September 2026 ([Considine, 2026](https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/documents/STM/2026-05/01_ceres-st-May2026-considine2.pdf)), but the review's pages show no result ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/earth-science/senior-review-2026/resources/)). FY2027 began October 1 under a CR (P.L. 119-103, to December 11, 2026) at FY2026 levels ([SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/house-clears-fy2027-cr-now-to-the-president/)), so GPM keeps operating for now. **Confidence: high.**

**GPM is the anchor IMERG can't easily replace.** IMERG uses GMI, calibrated to the DPR-GMI combined product, as the calibrator "for all the other satellite precipitation estimates" ([IMERG ATBD, Aug 2026](https://gpm.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IMERG_V07_ATBD_final_260813.pdf)). The same document's sensor table puts GPM's expected record at "Apr 14 - [Dec 29]", an estimate. NASA's orbit-boost page aims for "the early 2030's" and an overlap with AOS ([NASA](https://gpm.nasa.gov/missions/gpm/orbit-boost)), and JAXA says the boost extends GPM "into the early 2030s" ([Kubota, IPWG 2026](https://ipwg12.imgw.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/S2.3_Kubota-1.pdf)). A NASA Goddard white paper warns that JAXA's PMM "may be insufficient to serve as the next reference" because its radiometer lacks 19–37 GHz channels ([NASA GSFC, 2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260008217/downloads/PrecipWhitePaper_V4.pdf)). No NASA statement was found on how IMERG would be calibrated after GPM. **Confidence: moderate** (the end dates are estimates).

**The successor line has been cut back.** The FY2026 request said NASA "will not continue formulation of Earth System Observatory missions, including the Atmosphere Observing System (AOS) Storm and AOS-Sky". It also said INCUS "will not continue; NASA will close them out in FY 2026" ([NASA FY2026](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/fy-2026-budget-technical-supplement-002.pdf)). GAO reports that "PMM was previously known as AOS-Storm", that "the majority of AOS-Storm will not proceed", and that NASA's PMM role "now only includes science expertise and launch" ([GAO-26-108556](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108556/index.html)). NASA has since grouped "INCUS, PolSIR, PMM" into the FALCON fleet ([Considine, 2026](https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/documents/STM/2026-05/01_ceres-st-May2026-considine2.pdf)). FALCON's own radar is a W-band cloud radar "designed to observe shallow light precipitation", launching "no earlier than 2029" ([Intuitive Machines, Nov 3, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394137/0/en/intuitive-machines-selected-to-provide-spacecraft-platform-and-mission-solutions-for-nasa-s-falcon-radar-earth-science-mission.html)). That is not a replacement for GPM's DPR. INCUS survived in practice: in June 2026 two of its three SmallSats had "completed assembly and testing" for a 2027 launch ([NASA, Jun 5, 2026](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/smallsatellites/2026/06/05/nasas-incus-mission-on-road-to-launch-study-storms-from-space/)). **Confidence: moderate.**

**Congress has blunted the cuts so far.** FY2026 enacted Earth Science is $2.153B, against a $1,035.9M request ([FY2026 JES](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fy26_cjs_jes.pdf); [NASA FY2026](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/fy-2026-budget-technical-supplement-002.pdf)). For FY2027 the request is $1,021.2M ([NASA FY2027](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/fiscal-year-2027-full-budget-request.pdf)). The House bill gives $1.325B, which its minority calls a cut of "$828 million (38 percent)" ([H. Rept. 119-652](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-119hrpt652/html/CRPT-119hrpt652.htm)). The Senate committee gives $2,187,900,000 and is "concerned by the plan to end 53 missions across Science" ([Senate, 2026](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/cjs_explanatory_stmt.pdf)). It also questions consolidating "data processing, archive management" into a central Science Operations Center. **Confidence: high** for the figures.

**Goddard's people have gone; the data still flows.** NASA's OIG: "from March 2020 to January 2026, Goddard experienced a decrease of over 40 percent in total workforce. In 2025 alone, Goddard lost approximately a third of its civilian workforce due to the deferred resignation program" ([NASA OIG IG-26-005](https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/final-memorandum-ig-26-005-review-of-goddard-space-flight-centers-transformation-efforts.pdf)). GAO gives about 4,000 civil servants agency-wide, nearly 22 percent ([GAO-26-108556](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108556/index.html)). NASA headquarters slides plan to move the 11 data centers, GES DISC among them, "to thematic science enabling teams" with centralized cloud ingest and archive ([NASA ESDS, May 2026](https://espo.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2026-05/D2_0915_Scott_ESDS-Updates.pdf)). They list "Risks associated with data system consolidation" as a known issue ([NASA ESD town hall, Dec 2025](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/earth-science-division/resources/ESD%20Town%20Hall%2012162025.pdf)). PPS and GES DISC released GPM V08 in spring 2026 ([Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/pmm-pps-ges-disc-release-gpm-data-processed-under-algorithm-08)). IMERG V08 Final slipped to "the fall of 2026", and the Early/Late switch to "no sooner than winter of 2026" ([NASA GPM, Aug 2026](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/update-imerg-v08-transition-schedule-aug-2026)). The V07 Final record ends September 2025 ([NASA GPM](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/imerg-v08-transition-schedule)). No source links these delays to staffing or budget. **Confidence: high** for the staffing figures, **moderate** for the data-system plans.

**Alternatives would lack a radar reference and open data.** Tomorrow.io's two Ka-band radars are fixed-beam pathfinders whose retrieval is "trained on GPM combined radar-radiometer retrievals". NASA's evaluation got "Two months of non-continuous data obtained for fall 2023" under a "Public release EULA" ([NASA CSDA, Mar 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/tomorrow-io-radar-qa-sme-report-final-signed-1.pdf)). China's FY-3G carries a precipitation radar, with end of service "≥2029" ([NSMC](https://sac347.nsmc.org.cn/nsmc/en/satellite/FY3G.html)). JAXA's PMM radar is the only planned GPM-class radar found, and NASA's white paper doubts it can act as a reference alone. Microwave imagers and sounders from NOAA, EUMETSAT, JAXA and DoD feed IMERG but are calibrated to GPM. Their 2025–26 losses (Suomi NPP, AMSR2 near-real-time, DMSP uncertainty) are documented in the [earlier NOAA run](archive.html?id=noaa-satellites). **Confidence: moderate.**

## Conflicting evidence

- **Aqua end date (unresolved).** Status decks from Nov 2023 and May 2025 project "August 26, 2026, Projected Aqua Instrument Shutdown" and passivation on Nov 4, 2026 ([NASA, May 2025](https://aqua.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/AquaStatus_May2025.pdf)). NASA's May 2026 briefing gives "anticipated maximum lifetime: 9/2027" ([Considine, 2026](https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/documents/STM/2026-05/01_ceres-st-May2026-considine2.pdf)). The May 2025 deck ties September 21, 2027 to bus power under an over-guide request. Which applies now was not found. This doesn't affect IMERG.
- **GPM end of life.** The IMERG ATBD estimates December 2029; NASA's orbit-boost page and JAXA say the early 2030s. All are estimates.
- **Goddard workforce.** OIG: over 40% from March 2020 to January 2026 (total workforce). Scientific American: 40% from January 2025 to July 2026, 10,000 down to 6,000 including contractors ([SciAm, 2026](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/nasa-goddards-shrinking-workforce-could-stifle-future-space-missions-experts-say/)). GAO: a 34% civil-servant reduction at Goddard. These cover different periods and bases; this answer relies on the OIG.
- **Senior Review line for FY2027.** The FY2026 request shows the pooled Earth Science Senior Review line at $58.8M for FY2027; the FY2027 request shows $68.8M ([NASA FY2026](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/fy-2026-budget-technical-supplement-002.pdf); [NASA FY2027](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/fiscal-year-2027-full-budget-request.pdf)). These are two budget vintages; this answer uses the later one.
- **PMM launch.** WMO OSCAR (updated Oct 12, 2026) gives March 2030; an earlier OSCAR version, seen only as a search snippet, gave March 2029.
- **GPM orbit altitudes.** NASA gives 400 to 435 km, JAXA 407 to 442 km. Both describe a boost of about 35 km.

- **FY2027 appropriations:** a decision is due when the CR ends December 11, 2026.
- **How the INCUS closeout was reversed, and its FY2026 funding:** not found. NASA's FY2026 operating plan, which would show it, is not public.
- **PPS and GES DISC staffing and status during the Oct 1 – Nov 12, 2025 shutdown:** not found. All workforce figures are center- or agency-wide.
- **DAAC consolidation schedule and its effect on IMERG distribution:** not published.

- Executive orders affecting NASA science were not searched. Research ended at the token budget, not because the evidence ran out.
